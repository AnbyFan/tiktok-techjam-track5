Bottom Line

At 99.33% on a held-out validation set from the same distribution, your marginal accuracy gains will likely be small — the bigger payoff is in robustness to unseen generators and post-processing, which is where CLIP+linear-probe systems typically fail hardest and where competition judges often test. Below are approaches organized by where they'd help most.

Feature Engineering Beyond Global CLIP Embeddings

Your FFT attempt failing and mirror padding doing nothing suggests low-level pixel/frequency artifacts aren't adding signal on top of CLIP — which tracks with recent findings that CLIP-ViT linear probes already generalize better than frequency-domain approaches. Instead of raw FFT, try methods proven to complement CLIP:
arxiv

Multi-layer CLIP features: Instead of only the final pooled embedding, concatenate or attention-pool intermediate transformer block outputs (e.g., layers 6, 12, 18, 24 of ViT-L-14). Generative artifacts often show up at different semantic depths, and shallow-layer features can carry texture-level cues the final layer discards.

Neighboring Pixel Relationships (NPR): This is a specific, proven artifact representation (not raw FFT) that captures upsampling artifacts from generator architectures via pixel-difference maps, and it has been shown to generalize far better than naive frequency features to unseen GANs and diffusion models. It's a low-cost feature you haven't tried yet and is conceptually different from your failed FFT attempt.

+1

DIRE-style reconstruction error: Run the image through a diffusion model's inversion/reconstruction and use the reconstruction residual as an auxiliary feature — diffusion-generated images reconstruct with much lower error than real photos. This adds a completely orthogonal signal channel to CLIP semantics, though it's compute-heavy per image.
arxiv

Patch-based local CLIP features: Instead of one global embedding per image, extract CLIP features from multiple crops/patches and aggregate (mean, max, or attention pooling). This can catch localized artifacts in high-resolution composites that get diluted in a single global embedding.

Calibration Beyond Platt Scaling

Since you've already done Platt scaling and temperature scaling, consider:

Isotonic regression as a non-parametric alternative — it can capture non-monotonic miscalibration patterns Platt scaling's sigmoid assumption misses, though it needs more calibration data to avoid overfitting.

Beta calibration, which generalizes Platt scaling with an extra shape parameter and often outperforms it specifically for probabilistic classifiers with skewed score distributions like yours.

Conformal prediction for producing valid confidence intervals rather than point probabilities — useful for a competition since it gives you a principled way to flag low-confidence/ambiguous images rather than forcing a binary call, which can matter for scoring rubrics that reward abstention or calibrated uncertainty.

Data Augmentation for Robustness (Highest-Leverage Move)

This is the area most likely to move the needle, and it's distinct from what you've tried. Multiple 2024–2025 studies consistently find that training-time augmentation with realistic degradations — JPEG compression (quality 50–95), Gaussian blur, Gaussian noise, and resizing — substantially improves both cross-generator generalization and post-processing robustness, sometimes by double-digit accuracy points under compression. Notably, CLIP-based detectors trained with this augmentation become "basically insensitive" to JPEG/WebP compression and resizing, while unaugmented versions degrade. Since you haven't mentioned trying this, it's a strong next step:

Apply random JPEG compression (QF 50–95), Gaussian blur (σ 0.5–5), and mild Gaussian noise as on-the-fly augmentation during feature extraction/training.

Be careful about compression bias: apply augmentation to both real and fake images equally, or the model may just learn "JPEG artifacts = real" as a shortcut.
